# Jawb

A web app for college students applying to internships and jobs: keep one master resume, tailor a copy for each posting with AI suggestions you accept or reject, and track every application through to an offer. It's invite-only: you create invite codes and send them to the students you want to let in.

## Features

- **Dashboard** (`/`, where returning students land; a new account with an empty master resume goes to onboarding instead): the date and a greeting with a one-line summary ("4 things need you today · 3 jobs to tailor"), then four tinted tiles (To tailor, Applied, Interviews, Needs you), always shown, zeros included. Below them, in soft bordered panels: **Needs attention** (follow-ups due, with **Followed up**, and deadlines in the next 7 days); **In progress** (jobs still To apply and not sent, soonest deadline first), each with its company monogram, fit, deadline, a progress bar (suggested rewrites reviewed out of the latest analysis's total: accepted, edited past or rejected) or "Not analyzed yet"; then **All applications** with a segmented status filter (including Closing soon), the table (status, deadline, applied, follow-up, Discard on failed saves) and one card per job on phones. On the right: **Add a job**, a box for a job link or pasted description that grows with what you paste, with "Matches for you" below it (a template of empty job cards marked Coming soon), then **Pipeline**: a stacked bar and counts by status, and "Does tailoring help?" (interview rate of tailored vs untailored applications). A new account with no jobs sees a four-step **Get started** checklist instead of the empty lists. Layout: two columns from 1280px wide; between tablet and that, Add a job and Pipeline sit side by side under Needs attention; phones get one column. Shared panel pieces live in `src/components/dashboard/ui.tsx`. Progress is stored on the job (`suggestions_total`, `suggestions_pending`, migration 0006) by the job page's normal save, so the dashboard needs no resume reads.
- **Workspace**: on a laptop (1024px and wider) a left sidebar holds Dashboard, your master resume (pinned), your pinned jobs (synced to your account by migration 0009), then your last 8 other jobs by recent activity (with a status dot and fit), a search over all your jobs, **+ Add job**, and at the bottom your AI usage ("7 of 10 analyses left today"), Account and Sign out. The sidebar's button collapses it completely: the white canvas then fills the window and the button to bring it back sits in the canvas's top-left corner (the choice is kept in a cookie, so reloads don't flash). Below that width there's a slim header and the bottom tab bar (Dashboard, Master, Account). The master resume and every job open as a canvas split down the middle: the document editor on the left (the same toolbar, page and status bar on both), the AI panel on the right with its own tabs (job: **Overview**, **Posting**, **Letter**, **Facts**; master: **Overview**, **Facts**). On phones and tablets one tab row switches between **Resume** and the panel's tabs.
- **Onboarding**: with an empty master resume you get a full-page start instead of the editor, with three cards: paste your resume text, upload a PDF (both come in as they are, formatting included; no AI is used), or **I don't have a resume yet**. That opens the guided builder: (1) about you (name, email prefilled from your account, phone, city, links, school, degree, major, graduation date, optional GPA), (2) experiences and projects, as many as you like (type, organization or project name, role, dates, location, and "What did you actually do?" in a few sentences), (3) skills and tools. Each step checks what it needs before moving on, and your answers are kept on the device as you type (a refresh doesn't lose them). **Build my resume** (one of your daily analyses) makes a Harvard-format master resume: the header, education, entries, dates and skills come straight from your answers, and the AI only drafts each experience's bullets from what you wrote for it, with `[placeholders]` where a metric is missing (any number not in that experience's answer becomes a placeholder too). Your "what did you do" answers are saved as facts linked to their entries. You land in the editor with **Go deeper** on each experience, which opens the coaching questions for it.
- **Master resume**: a normal document editor (TipTap), like Google Docs: type, select and format anywhere. Toolbar and Format menu: undo/redo, font, size, bold/italic/underline, alignment, bulleted and numbered lists, indent, line and paragraph spacing, a line under a paragraph, horizontal line, clear formatting. There are no sections or line styles to manage: the app works out which lines are headings, entries and bullets from the text and its formatting (src/lib/doc/outline.ts), for the AI only. Importing a PDF keeps its fonts, sizes, bold/italic, alignment, bullets, right-aligned dates, lines and margins (read directly with pdf.js, no AI); pasted text comes in as is. Only the guided builder applies the Harvard template (Times New Roman). Re-importing keeps the ids of unchanged and lightly edited lines, so each job's "changes vs master" stays accurate.
- **Facts**: true things about you that the AI uses so it doesn't ask again (numbers, tools, side projects, answers you gave it, the bullets on your resume). Facts are rows in their own table (migration 0005) and are never printed, exported to PDF or put in the copy of what you sent. They come from: every bullet you import (linked to that bullet), answers to analysis questions, coaching answers and bullets you build with coaching, the guided builder, facts you add by hand, and facts the AI pulls out of your answers. The Facts panel lists every fact with a small label for where it came from; you can edit or delete any of them (with Undo). A fact linked to a bullet follows it: edit the bullet (on the master, or on a job page for a bullet you coached there) and the fact's text changes in the same save; empty or delete the bullet, or delete its job, and the fact stays on its own with its last wording. A fact that still has a `[placeholder]` is a draft the AI doesn't see; it becomes active by itself once the placeholder is filled. The AI sees only active facts, and never one that reads exactly like a bullet already on the resume it's looking at (so nothing is sent twice). For the number guard, a fact linked to one of the resume's own bullets only counts for that bullet. An answer is saved as a fact that reads on its own: for a question about a `[placeholder]` in a suggested bullet, the bullet sentence with your answer filled in (once all of that bullet's placeholders are answered); otherwise "topic: answer" (e.g. "Used SQL in any class or project: Yes, in DS 3000"). Facts from before 0005 were copied into the table as "Saved earlier" (the old JSON on the master is left as it was and no longer used).
- **Facts from your answers**: when you answer a coaching question, the AI splits the answer into short facts and saves them right away ("Saved 3 facts · Undo"). It may only restate what you wrote: the server drops any fact with a number your answer doesn't state, and skips duplicates of facts you already have.
- **Editing on two devices**: master resume saves never overwrite each other blindly. If the master changed somewhere else since this page loaded or last saved (another tab, your phone, "Save as master" or new facts from a job page), the app does a three-way merge against the version this page last saw: bullets, entry fields (title, second line, location, dates), the name and contact line, and section titles are matched by id, and whichever side changed something wins. Additions from either side are kept, and a deletion wins only if the other side didn't edit that line. (Facts are separate rows, so they never conflict.) When that works you see "Merged changes made on another device." If both sides changed the same line differently, autosave stops and a banner, "Your master resume was changed elsewhere", lists the lines and offers **Reload theirs** (drop this page's unsaved edits) or **Keep mine (overwrite)** (your version of those lines replaces theirs; their other changes are kept). Nothing is saved until you pick one. The job page works the same way: the tailored copy is three-way merged (or shows "This tailored copy was changed on another device"), job fields save only what you changed, answers merge, marking Applied never replaces a snapshot frozen on another device, and the page refreshes when you return to it. Once you've applied, the job page goes quiet; **Keep editing (for a re-apply)** opens it up again. The AI routes save their result only over the job as they read it: if it changed while the AI ran, `/api/analyze` re-reads it, keeps the answers given meanwhile and tries once more, and otherwise (and `/api/cover-letter` always) leaves the saving to the page, which merges. The job list's row edits are conditional too (see "Saving on a bad connection").
- **Add a job** (the dashboard's Jobs box): paste a link (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and other pages with JSON-LD fetch automatically) or paste the description. Links to private, local or internal network addresses are refused (every redirect is re-checked). LinkedIn, Handshake, Workday, Indeed, Glassdoor and Wellfound need a login or JavaScript, so for those you paste the description and the app keeps the link. If the job is already on the list (the same link, ignoring `?query`, `#hash` and a trailing slash, or the same company and title), you're asked "You already added this job (added Nov 13). Open it instead?" with **Open it** or **Add anyway**.
- **Per-job tailored copy**: each job gets its own copy of the master, analyzed on first open. Bullets are highlighted strong/okay/weak, suggested rewrites appear as tracked changes (accept or reject one at a time or all at once), and edits are marked against the master.
- **Coaching** (the panel to the right of the resume, on the master and on every job): select any entry or bullet and choose **Show me examples** or **Ask me questions first**. On a job, both are angled to the posting (its language and priorities); on the master they aim at what makes a bullet strong to any recruiter. Examples (up to 3 at a time, **More examples** for other angles) show the parts of a strong bullet (verb, scope, tool, result) and one line on why a recruiter cares, with a blank for every number. The server drops any example with a number outside a blank that isn't in your own bullet or facts; if one of your facts gives a blank's value, that blank is pre-filled and labeled "from your facts". **Use this bullet** opens a short step: for each blank you pick (nothing is preselected) the example value, your own, or a `[placeholder]`, see the finished bullet, then **Add to entry** or **Replace this bullet** (one undo step). Only then does anything reach your resume: the bullet goes on the page and becomes a fact linked to it (a draft until its placeholders are filled). Examples and their values stay in the page; they're never saved or sent to the AI again ("More examples" sends only a round number). **Ask me questions first** asks 3 to 5 questions with why each matters and how to estimate; answer roughly or skip. Your answers are saved as facts (plus the facts the AI pulls from them, "Saved 4 facts · Undo"), then **Write my bullet** brings examples with your numbers filled in. The analysis's own suggested rewrites (with `[placeholders]` and questions) work as before; coaching adds to them.
- **Overview (job pages)**: plain text on white that fades in: the job title (large, editable), company and location, a quiet line with status, deadline, follow-up, the posting link and a ⋯ menu (Re-analyze, Delete job); then the fit in one sentence ("You're a strong candidate…", "You have a good chance, but…", "Your resume isn't a close fit…") and the AI's verdict, word by word (once per analysis per page view; no motion with reduced motion); then the posting's keywords as small pills, faint red when your resume is missing one and faint green when it has it. Below that it stays blank until you click a bullet: then that bullet's feedback and suggested edit, its questions (answers become facts and fill the placeholder) and the coach appear there, and the panel scrolls to them. The master's Overview is the same, without the job parts. Suggested rewrites are reviewed in the document (tracked changes and the bullet's card). The **Posting** tab shows the description as a readable page with no AI involved (`formatPosting` in src/lib/postingFormat.ts turns pasted text into headings, paragraphs and lists, and links become clickable), with **Edit** for the link, description and notes; the **Letter** tab is the letter as plain editable text.
- **Fit bucket**: every analysis rates the job as a **Strong fit**, **Possible fit** or **Stretch**, with the verdict, matched and missing keywords, gaps and questions. The AI never invents numbers; it writes `[placeholders]` and asks you for the real value. The server checks this too (`guardNumbers` in `src/lib/analysis.ts`): a number in a suggested rewrite is kept only if it's in that same bullet, your facts or your answers, with the same unit (a `%` needs a `%`, `$` needs `$`, `3x` needs `3x`, `10K` needs 10,000). Numbers from other bullets, your contact line, dates, GPA or course codes don't count, and number words ("five", "a dozen", "twice", "doubled", "half") are checked the same way. Anything else becomes `[N]`, `[N]x`, `[X%]`, `[$X]`… with a question. In a cover letter, numbers from the job posting are kept only where the letter restates the posting, never in a sentence about you.
- **Deadlines**: give a job its application deadline; jobs closing soon are flagged on the list.
- **Submitted snapshot**: marking a job Applied (or jumping straight to Interview, Offer or Rejected) freezes an exact copy of the resume you sent (without your facts), sets today's date (your local day) and a 10-day follow-up, and records whether that tailored copy differed from the master resume. If the copy still has `[placeholders]` you're asked first. Moving the job back to To apply or Skipped offers to clear the submitted copy and Applied date. The job list and the job page share this logic (`applyStatusChange` in `src/lib/status.ts`). When a follow-up is due, the job list flags it and the row gets **Followed up**, which sets the next follow-up 7 days out (with **Undo**).
- **Outcomes**: the job list compares the interview rate of tailored and untailored applications (percentages appear once each group has 5 applications). A job counts as an interview if it reached Interview or Offer at any point, even if it later ended in Rejected (migration 0003; without it, only jobs currently in Interview or Offer count).
- **Saving on a bad connection**: every edit saves automatically. If a save fails (offline, a flaky phone connection), the change stays on screen, a "Not saved — retrying…" notice appears with **Retry now**, and the app retries after 3s, 10s, then every 30s and as soon as you're back online. On the job list, **Discard** next to a row puts back the saved value. Job list changes are also safe across devices: each write only lands if the job hasn't changed since this page last saw it; if it has (your phone and laptop both open), the job is re-read, the other device's changes show up, and only the fields you changed here are written again (`src/lib/jobWrites.ts`).
- **Invite-only accounts**: public sign-ups stay off in Supabase. A student opens `/signup` (or the "Create your account" link on the login page), enters their email, a password (8+ characters) and the invite code you gave them. The server checks the code and redeems one use atomically, creates the account with the service-role key (email marked confirmed, `app_metadata.invited = true`) and signs them in. If creating the account fails after the code was redeemed, the use is given back. Five wrong codes from one connection within 10 minutes block further tries for 10 minutes (IP addresses are stored only as hashes). Codes are 16 random base32 characters (`ABCD-EFGH-IJKL-MNOP`); single-use codes work once, reusable codes up to their limit, and either can expire. **Known tradeoff:** email ownership isn't verified at sign-up (the account is created confirmed), so someone with a code could register an address that isn't theirs; they still need the code, and the real owner can't be locked out of anything but that one account.
- **AI usage limits**: each student has two allowances, kept as numbers in the `ai_quotas` table (migration 0008). "Heavy" calls are job analyses, re-analyses, resume imports and builder drafts (default 10 per 24 hours). "Light" calls are quick AI actions: reading a pasted posting, cover letters, coaching and fact extraction (default 120 per hour). Edit a student's row in the Supabase Table Editor: `*_remaining` gives them calls right now, `*_limit` sets their total (lowering it also caps `*_remaining`), and `0` turns that kind of AI off for them. A window starts at their first call and refills when `*_resets_at` passes. New students get the column defaults. Each call takes one from `*_remaining` in the database before it runs (parallel requests can't slip past the cap), and a call that fails (the AI provider is down, say) isn't counted. Over the limit, the app says which limit and roughly when it frees up. The sidebar and the Account page show "7 of 10 analyses left today". Token counts are recorded for every call. Weights live in `AI_WEIGHTS` in `src/lib/aiBudget.ts`; `AI_HEAVY_PER_DAY` and `AI_LIGHT_PER_HOUR` are the fallback before 0008. Without migration 0004 the limits are off (the app warns in the server log).
- **Export**: ATS-friendly one-page PDF, plain-text copy, an optional cover letter draft, and a JSON backup of all your data (`GET /api/export`, a download of your resumes, jobs and facts). The backup is for safekeeping; there's no import, so restoring it is done by hand in Supabase.

Stack: Next.js 15 (App Router) · Supabase (Postgres + RLS + password/magic-link auth) · Google Gemini · @react-pdf/renderer · Tailwind v4 · Vitest.

## Setup

1. **Supabase**: create a project. In the SQL editor, run the files in `supabase/migrations/` in order:
   `0001_init.sql`, `0002_deadlines_snapshots.sql`, `0003_reached_interview.sql`, `0004_invites_usage.sql`,
   `0005_facts.sql`, `0006_progress.sql`, `0007_ai_quotas.sql`, `0008_ai_quota_counters.sql`, then `0009_job_nav.sql`. If you set the app up before, run only the ones you haven't run yet
   (each is safe to re-run except 0001). 0003 adds `jobs.reached_interview`, which remembers that an application got to
   the interview stage even after a later Rejected. 0004 adds invite codes, sign-up throttling and AI usage limits
   (`invite_codes`, `signup_attempts`, `ai_usage` and the `redeem_invite`, `release_invite`, `reserve_ai_call` and
   `finish_ai_call` functions). 0005 moves facts into a `facts` table and copies the master resume's existing facts
   into it (without 0005, facts can't be added and the AI uses the old list). 0006 adds `jobs.suggestions_total` and
   `jobs.suggestions_pending` for the dashboard's progress bars (without it, In progress shows "Analyzed" without a bar). The app keeps working without the later migrations and shows a "Database update
   needed" note where something depends on them (without 0004, sign-up with a code is unavailable and AI limits are off). 0008 turns each student's AI limits into the editable `ai_quotas` columns described above. 0009 keeps the sidebar's pinned jobs and recently-visited order in the account (the `job_nav` table), so they follow you across devices; without it they stay on each device.
2. **Auth redirect URLs**: under Authentication → URL Configuration, set the Site URL to your deployed URL
   (e.g. `https://your-app.vercel.app`) and add `https://your-app.vercel.app/**` and `http://localhost:3000/**`
   to the Redirect URLs. The magic link returns to `/auth/callback`, which these patterns cover.
3. **Gemini key**: https://aistudio.google.com/apikey
4. **Env vars**: copy `.env.example` to `.env.local` for local dev, and add the same keys in Vercel → Project → Settings → Environment Variables.
5. `npm install && npm run dev`
6. **Keep sign-ups off (required)**: go to Supabase → Authentication → Sign In / Providers and turn off "Allow new
   users to sign up". The anon key is public, so with sign-ups on anyone could create an account in your project.
   Students get accounts through `/signup` with an invite code instead (the server creates them with the service-role
   key, which isn't affected by that switch). Create your own account in Supabase → Authentication → Users → Add user
   and put your email on `ALLOWED_EMAILS`.
7. **Invite students**: create a code and send it (or the link `/signup?code=…`):

   ```bash
   node --env-file=.env.local scripts/create-invite.ts single - 14 "For Alex"
   ```

   ```bash
   node --env-file=.env.local scripts/create-invite.ts reusable 30 7 "CS club, fall cohort"
   ```

   Arguments: `single` or `reusable`, max uses (`-` for the default: 1 for single, 25 for reusable), days until it
   expires (`-` = never), and a note for yourself. It prints the code. Node 22.18+ runs the TypeScript file directly
   (older Node: add `--experimental-strip-types`). To look at or revoke codes, use the `invite_codes` table in Supabase
   (set `max_uses` to `uses` to stop a code).

Who can use the app: accounts created with an invite code (`app_metadata.invited`, read from the session on every
request with no database call) and emails on `ALLOWED_EMAILS`. Everyone else is signed out with "That account isn't
allowed to use this app". Note the change from before invites: an empty `ALLOWED_EMAILS` no longer lets every account in,
so put the owner's email there (or set `invited: true` in that user's app metadata).

| Variable | Notes |
|---|---|
| `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL` | Supabase → Project Settings → API |
| `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` | Publishable / anon key (safe in the browser; RLS protects data) |
| `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY` | Server-only (Supabase → Project Settings → API → service_role / secret key). Used only to redeem invite codes, throttle sign-ups and create invited accounts. Never commit it, never prefix it with `NEXT_PUBLIC_`; the app never logs it or sends it to the browser. |
| `ALLOWED_EMAILS` | Comma-separated emails allowed in without an invite (e.g. the owner's account made before invites). Invited accounts don't need to be listed. |
| `GEMINI_API_KEY` | Server-only. Never commit it. |
| `GEMINI_MODEL` | Optional, defaults to `gemini-3.8-flash` |
| `AI_HEAVY_PER_DAY` | Optional, default `10`: analyses, re-analyses, resume imports and builder drafts per student per rolling 24 hours. |
| `AI_LIGHT_PER_HOUR` | Optional, default `120`: coaching, fact extraction, cover letters and posting details per student per rolling 60 minutes. |

## Tests

```bash
npm test
```

Unit tests (Vitest, in `tests/`) cover resume normalization and id reconciliation, analysis normalization and merging, facts, fit buckets, outcome stats, paste-only sites, job-link fetching (timeouts, size cap, invalid links) and the private-address guard with a mocked network and DNS, the three-way master resume merge and conflict-safe saves with a fake Supabase client (`master.test.ts`), the facts table: mirroring linked facts on edit, emptied and deleted bullets, deleted jobs, drafts turning active, de-duplication, the legacy copy and answers saved as facts (`facts-table.test.ts`), fact extraction dropping invented numbers (`facts-table.test.ts`, `extract-facts-route.test.ts`), invite codes and the sign-up throttle (`invites.test.ts`), AI limits: the cap, parallel reservations, failed calls, heavy vs light windows (`ai-budget.test.ts`), coaching: example validation, every blank-resolution choice, inserting a bullet and its fact, and example text never reaching storage or a prompt (`coaching.test.ts`, `coach-routes.test.ts`), the builder: output normalization, builder facts and step validation (`builder.test.ts`), dashboard progress and sorting (`progress.test.ts`), sidebar search (`job-nav.test.ts`), local calendar dates, every branch of `applyStatusChange` and the duplicate-job check (`status.test.ts`), the number honesty guard (`honesty.test.ts`), the job list's write queue: retries, merging while a write is in flight, Discard, conflicts with another device and flushing when the page is hidden (`job-writes.test.ts`), duplicate checks in `/api/intake` and plain errors from `/api/export` (`intake-export.test.ts`), and the email allow-list in middleware and API routes (`auth.test.ts`). They need no Supabase or Gemini access.

## Security headers

`next.config.ts` sends `X-Frame-Options: DENY` and `Content-Security-Policy: frame-ancestors 'none'` (the app can't be framed), `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin`, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff` and a `Permissions-Policy` that turns off camera, microphone, location and similar APIs. There's deliberately no script CSP (Next's inline scripts would need nonces).
